## Phase 1: False Sharing Analysis

The experiment was conducted using 8 physical CPU cores
and 12 logical CPU threads.

The stride sensitivity experiment showed that the
lowest execution time occurred at stride=4 (32 bytes),
with a median of 1269.7716 ms.

Unexpectedly, execution time increased at stride=8
(64 bytes) and stride=16 (128 bytes).

The expected cache-line isolation improvement was
therefore not observed in this measurement.

The Karp-Flatt metric increased as the number of
processes grew, indicating growing parallel overhead
and reduced parallel efficiency.

Stride=16 generally demonstrated a lower Karp-Flatt
value than stride=1, suggesting better scaling.

Further measurements are required to isolate the
hardware false-sharing effect from process scheduling
and multiprocessing overhead.

### Phase 2 Analysis

The strong scaling experiment achieved an observed speedup of
7.0206 at 12 processes.

The theoretical Amdahl speedup at 12 processes was 11.6522.
The theoretical maximum speedup was 368.5128.

The weak scaling experiment showed increasing execution time
as the workload increased from 250,000 to 3,000,000 elements.

At 12 processes, Gustafson's scaled speedup was 11.6198,
while weak scaling efficiency was 0.2047.

Strong scaling results at p=3, p=4 and p=5 showed
efficiencies above 100%. These measurements are anomalous
and may indicate timing variability or measurement noise.
They should not be interpreted as verified superlinear scaling.
